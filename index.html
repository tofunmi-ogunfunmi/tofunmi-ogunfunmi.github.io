About me

Materials scientist exploring how solid-state batteries fail, and how to make them last.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Materials Science & Engineering at UC Berkeley, where I work at the National Center for Electron Microscopy at Lawrence Berkeley National Lab with Dr. Mary Scott.

I use electron microscopy methods to understand solid-state battery materials: why electrolytes short and crack, and what makes them more durable.

Research

My research connects the solid-state electrolyte microstructure to the overall battery performance, using electron microscopy methods (e.g. SEM/EDS, TEM/STEM, 4D-STEM, EELS, FIB-SEM tomography).

FIB-SEM tomography

A focused ion beam (FIB) removes a slice of material a few tens of nanometres thick, then the scanning electron microscope (SEM) images the freshly exposed face. Repeating this hundreds of times gives a stack of images that can be segmented and reconstructed into a 3D structur. Here the sample is a solid electrolyte containing lithium filaments (green), voids (blue) and Ag-rich particles (amber).

4D-STEM

A focused electron probe is scanned across the sample and a 2D diffraction pattern is recorded at every probe position. Afterwards, any "virtual detector" can be applied in software to reconstruct an image.

EELS

As electrons pass through the sample, some lose energy. In the energy-loss spectrum each element shows an absorption edge at a characteristic energy, and the shape of the edge reflects bonding. Here the probe sits on a Li metal | SEI | LLZO (solid-electrolyte) interface.
